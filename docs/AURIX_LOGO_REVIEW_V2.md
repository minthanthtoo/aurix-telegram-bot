# AuriX Logo Review and V2 Direction

Version: 2.0 review  
Status: V2 recommended for refinement and clearance  
Reviewed assets: V1 mark, horizontal lockups, reverse lockup, and Facebook cover

## Executive judgment

The V1 system had the right strategic ingredients—an A gateway, an X signal, and an aurora palette—but the symbol did not yet feel ownable. The X sat across the A like an added ribbon, the rounded construction leaned playful, and the cover behaved like a campaign advertisement instead of a durable page identity.

V2 keeps the strongest idea and rebuilds the relationship: the A and X now share a structural diagonal. The result is more compact, more intentional, easier to recognize as an AX monogram, and better aligned with a calm, trustworthy technology service.

## Evidence and scope

This is a formal design critique of the supplied AuriX brand assets in this repository. It evaluates silhouette, hierarchy, legibility, strategic fit, extensibility, and likely social-media behavior. It is not trademark or confusing-similarity clearance.

## V1 observations

### What worked

- The dark field and aurora gradient established a modern digital-service atmosphere.
- Gold introduced warmth and prevented the identity from becoming cold or anonymous.
- The A-plus-X concept connected directly to the AuriX name.
- The squircle container worked naturally as a Facebook and Telegram avatar.

### What weakened the identity

- The X appeared placed on top of the A rather than generated from the same idea. This made the mark feel assembled instead of resolved.
- The broad rounded strokes and oversized intersection read as soft and slightly toy-like, reducing the sense of operational trust.
- At small size, the overlap risked reading as a shoelace, bandage, or decorative ribbon.
- The wordmark depended on a generic heavy sans serif without enough relationship to the symbol.
- The cover mixed identity, offer language, and an imitation CTA. A page cover should support recognition while Facebook's native action button carries the click behavior.

## V2 design decision

### Core mechanism

The right leg of the A is also one arm of the X. A second signal stroke crosses it at a controlled woven junction. This makes the connection concept structural rather than decorative.

### Formal improvements

- Cleaner outer silhouette with fewer competing contours.
- Narrower strokes and a smaller crossing produce a more precise, capable tone.
- Gold remains attached to the gateway; cyan and violet emerge through the connection path.
- The short pale crossbar preserves the A reading without dominating the signal.
- The refined squircle remains native to profile-picture use.
- The wordmark uses tighter spacing and a gradient X to echo the monogram without over-styling every letter.
- The V2 cover removes the artificial button and becomes a stable brand header.

## Transferable brand principles

1. **Connection must be structural.** When the brand visualizes access or signal, paths should join, pass through, or reveal—not float as decoration.
2. **Warm gateway, cool signal.** Gold represents welcoming access; cyan and violet represent movement and technology.
3. **Calm confidence over cyber spectacle.** Use depth and contrast sparingly. Avoid hacker grids, shields, locks, flags, and excessive glow.
4. **One dominant gesture.** Keep the shared AX stroke recognizable across avatars, posts, motion, and UI moments.
5. **Native platform behavior.** Design covers as identity surfaces and use Facebook's actual CTA controls for action.

## Anti-imitation boundaries

- Do not add a separate X badge, crossing ribbon, or floating spark over the mark.
- Do not turn the monogram into a shield, keyhole, lightning bolt, or superhero emblem.
- Do not introduce additional gradients inside the same small symbol.
- Do not trace or imitate another VPN company's lock, globe, animal, or tunnel iconography.
- Do not rely on glow to make weak geometry feel distinctive.

## Production guidance

- Use the mark alone for Facebook and Telegram profile images.
- Keep the horizontal lockup at 140 px wide or larger.
- Keep the mark at 32 px or larger; use a dedicated simplified favicon below 32 px.
- Keep clear space around the complete logo equal to roughly one quarter of the mark width.
- Export Facebook profile art at 1080 × 1080 and the cover at 1640 × 624, then inspect both desktop and mobile crops.
- Convert the approved live wordmark to vector outlines before sending production files to third parties.
- Complete name, handle, domain, and trademark clearance before launch.

## Motion behavior

For motion posts, reveal the gateway first in gold, let the cool signal travel through the shared diagonal, and finish with the wordmark. Keep the full logo reveal between 0.8 and 1.4 seconds. Avoid spinning, electrical glitches, or continuous pulsing; one clean pass reinforces clarity and reliability.
