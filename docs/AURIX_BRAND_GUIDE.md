# AuriX Brand and Social Design System

Version: 2.0 draft  
Market: Myanmar  
Primary channel: Facebook  
Conversion channel: Telegram  
Status: Creative reference; offer claims and Burmese copy require approval before paid publication

## 1. Brand summary

AuriX is a Myanmar-first, Telegram-assisted VPN service built around transparent data entitlements, simple activation, and human support.

The brand must feel modern and capable without looking anonymous, aggressive, political, or technically intimidating. It should make a first-time VPN buyer feel that the service is understandable and that a real person will help if something goes wrong.

### Brand idea

**A clear gateway to reliable access.**

### Core promise

**Simple, transparent VPN access with human help when you need it.**

### Audience takeaway

After one exposure, the viewer should remember:

> AuriX is the easy-to-understand VPN I can start and manage through Telegram.

### Primary tagline

**Clear access. Human help.**

### Primary CTA

**Start on Telegram**

Do not make Facebook itself the service interface. Facebook creates recognition and trust; Telegram handles trial, order, receipt, support, and access delivery.

## 2. Product truth and launch gate

Brand communication must follow the product, not outrun it.

### Facts currently represented in the application

- Public starter access: 300 MB, renewable once per rolling 24 hours.
- Monthly free access: 3 GB for 30 days, renewable every rolling 30 days.
- Paid plans: 50 GB for 30 days at 3,000 MMK; 100 GB for 30 days at 6,000 MMK.
- Payment review: receipt submission followed by staff verification.
- Delivery and support: Telegram-first.
- Entitlement: time plus pooled bandwidth per key; not a physical-device identity.
- VPN implementation: Outline-backed access keys.

### Publication states

| State | What may be published |
|---|---|
| Brand preview | Brand story, education, waitlist, behind-the-scenes, Telegram community CTA |
| Controlled test | Invitation-only tester posts with explicit test language |
| Public offer | Starter, trial, price, quota, and activation claims only after live Telegram/Outline acceptance testing |
| Paid acquisition | Only after capacity, support ownership, payment workflow, privacy, refund, and abuse rules are approved |

The code repository is not evidence that the live service is ready. Before publishing an offer, confirm the bot, server, capacity, payment method, support path, and exact terms in production.

## 3. Strategic territory selection

Four territories were considered:

1. **Aurora Gateway — selected.** Light becomes a doorway and data path. Distinctive to the AuriX name, optimistic, premium, and easy to animate.
2. **Signal Pulse.** Communicates connectivity but resembles generic telecom and speed-test branding.
3. **Golden Key.** Connects to access and the “Auri” sound, but overuses security clichés and implies possession more than service.
4. **Human Relay.** Strong for concierge support, but difficult to own as a scalable technology identity.

### Concept lock

- Core promise: clear VPN access with human support.
- Visual mechanism: a luminous A-shaped gateway crossed by an X signal.
- Emotional lane: calm confidence, not fear.
- Product memory: transparent quota, duration, and Telegram help.
- CTA role: move an interested Facebook viewer into Telegram.

### We are not

- Hacker-themed
- Fear-based

- Loudly “crypto”
- Politically symbolic
- Overly luxurious

### Voice formula

**Direct fact + simple benefit + human next step.**

Example:

Avoid:

## 5. Audience

### Primary audience

Myanmar mobile-first users who:

- discover products through Facebook;
- prefer Telegram for private conversation and delivery;
- want a VPN without complex setup language;
- are price-aware and need clear limits;
- value access to a real support person;
- may be using a VPN for the first time.

### Secondary audience

- Existing VPN users dissatisfied with unclear plans or poor support.
- Students, freelancers, remote workers, and mobile entertainment users.
- Early testers who can provide connection and usage evidence.

### Audience anxiety to resolve

- “Will it work on my phone?”
- “How much data do I receive?”
- “How long does it last?”
- “How do I pay?”
- “Who helps me if setup fails?”
- “Is ‘unlimited’ real?”

AuriX answers with visible terms, short steps, and a named support path.

## 6. Logo system

### Logo concept

The refined mark combines:

- an **A-shaped gateway**, representing entry and upward movement;
- an **X signal sharing the A's right diagonal**, representing connection, exchange, and the name ending;
- a **midnight field**, representing a calm digital environment;
- an **aurora gradient**, representing access becoming visible and understandable.

The shared diagonal is the key distinctive gesture. It makes connection part of the letter structure rather than a decorative X placed over the A.

### Master assets

- [Logo mark V2](../brand/v2/aurix-logo-mark-v2.svg)
- [Horizontal logo V2](../brand/v2/aurix-logo-horizontal-v2.svg)
- [Horizontal reverse logo V2](../brand/v2/aurix-logo-horizontal-reverse-v2.svg)
- [Facebook cover V3 — mobile-safe master](../brand/v3/facebook-cover-mobile-safe-v3.svg)
- [Logo critique and V2 rationale](AURIX_LOGO_REVIEW_V2.md)

The horizontal SVG uses live type for editability. Convert the final approved wordmark to vector outlines before trademark filing, print production, or handoff to external vendors.

This is an original design concept, not legal clearance. Complete name, trademark, domain, Facebook-handle, and confusing-similarity checks before investing in a public launch.

### Clear space

Keep clear space around the logo equal to the width of the X signal in the mark. Do not let a headline, face, platform badge, or edge enter this area.

### Minimum sizes

- Digital logo mark: 32 px minimum.
- Horizontal logo: 140 px minimum width.
- Profile avatar: use only the logo mark.
- Small favicon or Telegram avatar: simplify to the A gateway if the X signal becomes illegible.

### Incorrect use

- Do not add shields, locks, wings, flags, maps, or crowns.
- Do not stretch or rotate the mark.
- Do not recolor it with random gradients.
- Do not put the horizontal logo over a busy photograph.
- Do not use glow so heavily that the silhouette loses clarity.
- Do not replace the X with a multiplication sign or close icon.

## 7. Color system

### Primary palette

| Token | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Midnight Ink | `#071421` | Primary dark background and dark text |
| Deep Space | `#0D2235` | Secondary background and panels |
| Aurora Cyan | `#36E2FF` | Signal, connection, highlight |
| Signal Violet | `#7765FF` | Technology accent and gradient end |
| Auri Gold | `#FFC857` | Brand warmth, CTA, value highlight |
| Cloud | `#F6F8FC` | Light background and reverse text |
| Slate | `#526273` | Secondary text |

### Functional palette

| Token | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Success Mint | `#42D392` | Confirmed success only |
| Warning Amber | `#FFB547` | Pending or review state |
| Alert Coral | `#FF6B6B` | Failure, interruption, or important warning |

### Usage ratio

- 60% Midnight Ink or Cloud.
- 25% Deep Space or neutral support color.
- 10% Aurora Cyan/Signal Violet.
- 5% Auri Gold for focus and CTA.

Gold should signal value or action. Cyan should signal connection or movement. Do not make every element glow.

### Accessibility rule

- Use Cloud on Midnight Ink for body text.
- Use Midnight Ink on Auri Gold for CTA labels.
- Do not use Aurora Cyan for small text on Cloud.
- Verify contrast in the final export, not only in the design file.

## 8. Typography

### Recommended families

- Latin and numerals: **Inter**.
- Burmese: **Noto Sans Myanmar**.
- Fallback Burmese: **Padauk**.

### Hierarchy

- Display: ExtraBold, short, 1–2 lines.
- Headline: Bold, one clear promise.
- Supporting copy: Medium or Regular.
- Price/quota numerals: ExtraBold with generous spacing.
- CTA: Bold, sentence case or concise uppercase English.

### Mobile readability

- Prefer one headline, one supporting line, and one CTA.
- Keep important text away from the outer 8–10% of the canvas.
- Avoid more than three text sizes in one post.
- Do not embed text in generated imagery; add typography in the design tool.

## 9. Visual language

### Signature elements

- A-shaped luminous gateways.
- Smooth aurora ribbons.
- One clear signal path crossing an environment.
- Rounded cards and portals.
- Soft volumetric light with crisp geometry.
- Dark-to-light transitions that represent clarity.
- Human-scale mobile moments when people are shown.

### Composition

- One dominant focal object.
- Strong negative space reserved for copy.
- Clear foreground, midground, and background separation.
- Mobile-readable silhouette.
- Minimal interface chrome.
- Controlled glow and realistic material response.

### Photography and people

Use contemporary Myanmar contexts without reducing the market to stereotypes. Show ordinary, capable people in modern homes, cafés, study areas, or small workspaces. Keep wardrobe neutral and current. Avoid political symbols, religious sites used decoratively, exoticized rural imagery, and generic “Asian tech” casting.

Any culturally specific image or Burmese copy requires local human review before paid use.

### Avoided visual clichés

- Padlocks and shields.
- Hooded hackers.
- Matrix code.
- Flags wrapped around servers.
- Glowing globe with random network lines.
- Speedometer needles.
- Fake phone interfaces.
- Fear imagery about surveillance.
- Cyberpunk overload.

## 11. Content system

### Content pillars

1. **Product clarity — 30%.** Plans, quotas, durations, setup, renewals.
2. **Education and trust — 25%.** How VPN keys work, pooled data, safe setup, honest limits.
3. **Offer and conversion — 25%.** Approved trial, plan, or tester invitation.
4. **Human operations — 20%.** Support, payment review, maintenance updates, real testimonials with consent.

### Recurring series

- **AuriX Clear Plan** — one plan fact per post.
- **One-Minute VPN** — one simple educational idea.
- **Ask AuriX** — support-led FAQ.
- **Signal Check** — transparent operations or maintenance update.
- **Start Simple** — trial/onboarding sequence.

### Static-post anatomy

```text
Visual hook
  → short factual headline
  → one supporting detail
  → one CTA
  → small terms line when required
```

### Carousel anatomy

```text
Card 1: recognizable question or promise
Card 2: simple explanation
Card 3: product fact or proof
Card 4: action in Telegram
```

### Motion system

- Runtime: 6–12 seconds for first tests.
- Shots: 3–5.
- Hook: first frame must already show the gateway, signal, phone, or plan object.
- Mechanism: one portal opening, one signal ribbon, or one clear before/after transition.
- Text and logo: add in post for legibility.
- End frame: hold logo, factual offer, and CTA for at least 1.5 seconds.
- Loop: let the closing aurora path return to the opening gateway shape.
- Audio: calm electronic pulse with restrained Myanmar-inspired texture only after cultural review; do not use a generic “ethnic” music trope.

### First three motion concepts

1. **Gateway Open — 8 seconds.** A dark A silhouette catches one cyan signal; the portal opens to soft light; the signal travels through; logo and Telegram CTA hold for 1.5 seconds.
2. **Clear Plan — 10 seconds.** Three abstract cards appear; one plan is selected with a gold ring; quota and duration typography enter in post; the selected card becomes an A gateway.
3. **One Shared Allowance — 8 seconds.** One central data reservoir branches to phone, tablet, and laptop; each branch draws from the same visible source; the branches return to the source to form a natural loop.

Use generated motion for picture only. Add exact offer copy, Burmese text, logo, terms, and CTA during post-production.

### Number and offer style

- Use decimal `300 MB`, `3 GB`, `50 GB`, and `100 GB` when describing the implemented limits; the code stores 300,000,000 / 3,000,000,000 / 50,000,000,000 / 100,000,000,000 bytes.
- Use `24 hours` and `30 days`; do not call a rolling 30-day Outline limit a calendar-month reset.
- Format prices as `3,000 MMK` and `6,000 MMK` in English and approved local equivalents in Burmese.
- Keep price, quota, duration, and eligibility together.

## 13. Claims policy

### Allowed after operational verification

- Exact quota and duration.
- Exact price.
- Telegram-assisted activation.
- Human receipt review.
- Support availability during published support hours.
- One key’s data allowance may be shared across devices, if terms permit.

### Requires evidence before use

- Fast or low-latency.
- Reliable or stable.
- Works on a named device/platform.
- Immediate activation.
- Immediate disconnection at quota or expiry.
- Specific uptime or response time.
- Privacy or logging claims.

### Prohibited without a fundamental product/policy change

- Unlimited.
- World’s fastest.
- 100% anonymous.
- Untraceable.
- Military-grade.
- Zero logs.
- Guaranteed access everywhere.
- One Telegram account equals one physical device.
- Guaranteed protection from all threats.

## 14. Production workflow

1. Select one content pillar and one business objective.
2. Confirm the offer/claim state against live operations.
3. Choose a prompt from `AURIX_SOCIAL_PROMPT_LIBRARY.md`.
4. Generate only the visual plate—no text or logo.
5. Add approved type, logo, CTA, and terms in the design tool.
6. Review mobile readability at 25% zoom.
7. Run brand, claim, Burmese-language, and cultural checks separately.
8. Export and archive the source, approved copy IDs, prompt ID, and publication date.

### Naming convention

```text
AURIX_[PILLAR]_[CAMPAIGN]_[FORMAT]_[LANG]_[VERSION]
```

Example:

```text
AURIX_OFFER_BASIC50_4X5_MY_V03
```

## 15. Measurement

Prioritize business evidence over vanity metrics.

- Facebook post → Telegram click rate.
- Telegram start → trial claim rate.
- Trial → paid order rate.
- Receipt → approved activation time.
- Activation failure and support-contact rate.
- 30-day renewal rate.
- Cost per activated paid customer.
- Actual data usage by customer percentile.

Creative metrics such as thumb-stop rate, saves, shares, and completion rate are diagnostic inputs, not the final goal.
