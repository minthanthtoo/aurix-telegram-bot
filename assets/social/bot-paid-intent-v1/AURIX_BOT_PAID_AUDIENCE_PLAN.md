# AuriX Bot Paid-Intent Introduction — Audience and Creative Plan

Date: 2026-08-30
Format: Facebook portrait static, 1080 × 1350
Conversion destination: `@aurix_outline_vpn_bot`

## Audience study

### Primary segment

Myanmar mobile-first professionals who already understand why they need a VPN and are willing to pay to reduce purchase and setup friction:

- freelancers and remote workers who cannot spend long comparing free tools;
- online sellers and creators whose work depends on access to communication and publishing tools;
- researchers and office users who need predictable quota, duration, and a support path;
- existing VPN users dissatisfied with unclear limits, difficult payment, or faceless support.

Their buying questions are practical: `How quickly can I start?`, `Can I pay with my usual wallet?`, `What exactly do I receive?`, and `Who helps if the key does not work?` Free access is useful as a low-risk proof point, but it must not dominate a paid-intent message.

### Current market signals

- DataReportal reports that 52.6% of Myanmar's internet-user base used at least one social platform in October 2025, supporting a Facebook-to-Telegram acquisition path: <https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-myanmar>
- The World Bank reports that VPN restrictions and connectivity disruption have increased business costs while digital-payment modernization continues: <https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099120625204042781/pdf/P507203-7c4662b6-c1d8-4c3c-9b0c-d4835f2763cb.pdf>
- Reporting in 2025 noted that free VPN availability had declined and paid services had emerged. This supports a professional, continuity-oriented audience rather than free-only positioning: <https://www.rfa.org/english/myanmar/2025/01/10/myanmar-internet-freedom/>
- A 2025 Yangon export/import-professional survey found strong use of KBZPay and WaveMoney and broader use of local digital payments, supporting visible familiar-wallet recognition: <https://meral.edu.mm/record/11693/files/Ngun%20Bawi%20MPA-51%2C%2022nd%20Batch%20%2818-7-2025%29.pdf>

These signals guide audience framing only. The creative does not use political, circumvention, anonymity, or legal-safety claims.

## Commercial brief

- Audience tension: paid-intent users need to begin work without a slow, confusing buying process.
- Objective: introduce the AuriX Telegram Bot as the direct paid purchase and key-delivery path.
- Primary promise: choose a plan, send a receipt, and receive an Outline-compatible key after staff verification.
- Product memory: `AuriX Bot · usually under-one-minute receipt review · Official Outline Client`.
- Reason to believe: transparent plan facts, five familiar wallets, staff-assisted review, and a named Bot destination.
- Free role: secondary safety net for anyone who wants to test first.
- Primary metric: qualified Bot starts and paid-plan orders.

## Territory exploration

1. Bot chat screenshot — rejected: resembles a website/app screen, creates fake-control risk, and ages quickly.
2. Generic server room with shield — rejected: generic AI/VPN cliché and unsupported security implication.
3. Human-at-laptop photograph — rejected for this asset: visually familiar but weak at proving the Bot flow.
4. **Editorial service relay — selected:** AuriX Bot avatar, a large time signal, receipt-review steps, Outline Client anchor, plan facts, and an open wallet row. It demonstrates the service without fake UI.

## Plan council and improvement

The first plan tried to give equal weight to the free allowance, paid plans, speed, server reliability, payments, and setup. The council judged that this would attract free users and dilute the Bot introduction. The revised plan makes paid purchase speed the hero, keeps plans and wallets as conversion proof, and demotes the free 300 MB offer to one compact safety-net line. Reliability is conveyed through an explicit process and support destination rather than an unverified adjective.

## Gemini localization record

The Burmese draft was reviewed twice through local 9Router using `ag/gemini-3.7-flash-high`. Gemini's strongest retained phrase was `ပုံမှန်ဆို ပြေစာပို့ပြီး ၁ မိနစ်မပြည့်ခင် စစ်ပေးပါတယ်*`. Formal constructions and a line implying smooth network performance were rejected. The final remains evidence-checked by the campaign workflow rather than treating model output as authoritative.

## Publication gates

- Measure receipt submission-to-human-verification time under real staffing and confirm that “usually under one minute” remains true.
- Confirm both paid plans and all five recipient wallets are live.
- Run customer data-plane tests across representative Myanmar ISPs before using `reliable`, `stable`, or uninterrupted-access claims.
- Review applicable local rules before paid publication; this creative does not offer legal or safety assurances.
