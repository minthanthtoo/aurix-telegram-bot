# AuriX Pricing Exploration V2

Date: 2026-08-29  
Reason for reopening: the first final set was readable but resembled pricing UI placed over generic glossy AI technology art.

## What failed in V1

- Rounded panels, even when not buttons, borrowed too much visual grammar from a website pricing section.
- Cyan/violet glass vessels and neon gateways looked polished but predictable and synthetic.
- The composition explained the offer but did not create a strong Facebook-feed interruption.
- Both styles were variations of the same dark-tech aesthetic instead of genuinely different campaign ideas.
- The audience saw a plan table before feeling a relevant situation or distinctive point of view.

V1 remains an internal exploration only. It is no longer recommended for publication.

## Five explored territories

### 1. Street-print price poster

Bold screen-printed paper, imperfect ink registration, torn edges, oversized numerals, and physical paste texture. It feels made by a designer, not exported from a web component library. Strongest for feed interruption and price memory.

### 2. Independent newspaper spread

High-contrast editorial columns, annotations, and a restrained monochrome palette. Credible but risks feeling like a policy announcement rather than an ad.

### 3. Lived-in documentary diptych

Two honest everyday Myanmar usage moments: mobile-first casual use and a heavier work/study setup. Pricing is assigned to the scenes with open editorial typography, not cards. Strongest human relevance.

### 4. Market-board sign painting

Hand-painted price-board energy with physical brush lettering and local color. Distinctive, but generated Burmese lettering would be unreliable and could become culturally performative.

### 5. Pure kinetic typography

No generated imagery—only huge quota/price typography, crop, rhythm, and brand color. Highly legible but offers less emotional connection and may become repetitive across a campaign.

## Council selection

### Selected A — Street Print

- Why: highest stopping power, strongest departure from AI-gloss, and excellent numeric memory.
- Visual mechanism: two overlapping physical poster sheets; `50 GB` and `100 GB` act as the image rather than sitting inside cards.
- Risk: distressed texture can reduce Burmese legibility. Mitigation: keep all exact copy in clean deterministic type over quieter paper zones.

### Selected B — Real Life, Two Rhythms

- Why: shows who the plans are for without inventing technical performance claims.
- Visual mechanism: photographic diptych—phone-first everyday use versus laptop/phone heavier use—with one shared signal-colored seam.
- Risk: stock-photo or AI-human feel. Mitigation: observational framing, partial profiles/hands, imperfect real environments, natural mixed light, no exaggerated smiles, no impossible interfaces.

## Locked content hierarchy

The content strategy stays accurate, but the presentation changes from “plan cards” to “two choices in one poster.”

1. Hook: `VPN သုံးတာ ဘယ်လောက်များလဲ။`
2. Choice A: `50 GB · ရက် 30 · 3,000 ကျပ်` with cue `ပုံမှန်သုံးသူ`.
3. Choice B: `100 GB · ရက် 30 · 6,000 ကျပ်` with cue `ပိုသုံးသူ`.
4. Free entry, treated as a margin note: `အရင်စမ်းချင်ရင် — နေ့စဉ် 300 MB · ရက် 30 တိုင်း 3 GB အခမဲ့`.
5. Product truth: `AuriX key · Official Outline Client ဖြင့် အသုံးပြုရန်`.
6. Destination, not a button: `@aurix_outline_vpn_bot`.

## Reject conditions

- Any rounded pricing box, pill, button, dashboard, browser, or app-screen composition.
- Glossy glass capsules, generic neon server rooms, cyberpunk lighting, shields, locks, or hacker imagery.
- Generated Burmese text or fake interface content.
- Lifestyle photography that looks staged, luxurious, Western-generic, or euphoric.
- “Best,” “popular,” “unlimited,” discount, speed, or security claims.

## Publishing sequence

Lead with Street Print for reach and price memory. Use Real Life, Two Rhythms as the follow-up for relevance and conversion. Compare bot starts per 1,000 reached users rather than likes alone.
